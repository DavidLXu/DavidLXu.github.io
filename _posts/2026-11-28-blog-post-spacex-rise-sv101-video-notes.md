As SpaceX approaches what could be the largest IPO in history, this in-depth documentary by **[Silicon Valley 101 (硅谷101)](https://www.youtube.com/@TheValley101)** (*[The Rise of SpaceX: Everything for Mars — Visiting Starbase and Headquarters](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x2meJPOn9ws)*) invites former SpaceX executive and Falcon 9 chief engineer Lewis Hong to revisit the Los Angeles warehouse and headquarters where the company took off, along with the Starbase Starship facility at the southern tip of Texas.

Below is a detailed breakdown of the entire episode organized by timeline and core themes.

---

## 1. Founding Origins: From "Performance Art" to Falcon 1 in a Small Warehouse

* **"Mars Oasis" and humiliation in Russia:** SpaceX began with an idea bordering on performance art. Browsing NASA's website, Elon Musk was stunned to find no timeline for returning to the Moon—let alone landing on Mars. His original plan, "Mars Oasis," was to buy a retired Soviet ICBM and land a small greenhouse on Mars to snap a photo that would stir public excitement and pressure the U.S. government into boosting NASA's budget. He flew to Russia three times to negotiate, only to be quoted extortionate prices; one Russian rocket designer even spat on his shoes, convinced the rich American knew nothing about rocketry.
* **First-principles math on the flight home:** On the plane back to the U.S., Musk built an Excel spreadsheet tallying market prices for aluminum alloys, titanium, carbon fiber, propellants, and other raw rocket materials—and discovered raw materials accounted for only about **3%** of a rocket's selling price. He realized astronomical launch prices stemmed less from pure physics than from bureaucratic inefficiency, bloated supply chains, and the "cost-plus" contracting habits of legacy defense primes like Boeing and Lockheed Martin. In 2002—even earlier than Facebook—he used his PayPal payout to found SpaceX.
* **A tiny warehouse and survival on a remote atoll:** The crew visited SpaceX's very first small warehouse near Los Angeles International Airport (now a seafood trading company). Lewis recalled that the company literally started with Musk and employee #1, famed propulsion engineer Tom Mueller, developing the entire Falcon 1 rocket inside that modest shop. Launch tests took place on a 0.03-square-kilometer island in the Marshall Islands in the Pacific, with no fresh water and no proper dormitories; heavy salt spray forced engineers to constantly clean and inspect metal hardware.

---

## 2. Three Straight Falcon 1 Failures and Survival at the Edge (2006–2008)

Musk's initial strategy was to start with a small rocket for small satellites and iterate upward. Yet Falcon 1—small enough to look almost like a toy—kept the team fighting for survival for years:

1. **First failure (March 2006):** Crashed dozens of seconds after liftoff. Extreme tropical salt corrosion created a micro-crack in a fuel-line nut, causing a leak and engine shutdown. Lewis admitted the team truly did not know how to build rockets yet—but learned extraordinarily fast, immediately redesigning the entire first-stage propulsion plumbing.
2. **Second failure (March 2007):** The first stage performed flawlessly, yet after stage separation the second stage suffered attitude-control trouble and violent slosh/oscillation, shutting down early before reaching orbit.
3. **Third failure (August 2008):** The most heartbreaking loss. Every earlier issue had been fixed, only to trip on separation timing—second-stage separation fired one second late, allowing residual thrust from the first stage to bump the second stage. The rocket tumbled into the Pacific along with NASA small satellites and the cremated remains of 208 people booked for a space burial. As Lewis reflected, the third rocket was already a 99% finished vehicle—yet in aerospace, 99% is nowhere near enough; it has to be 100%.

* **The darkest hour and an impromptu speech:** After three consecutive failures, Musk's PayPal proceeds were nearly gone—compounded by the 2008 financial crisis, Tesla teetering on bankruptcy, and a bitter divorce. Control room silence gave way to open tears. Musk delivered an impromptu speech promising, "I will never give up, never," announced he had scraped together funding for a fourth flight, and urged everyone back to their desks to find the root cause.
* **Two lifelines and a historic first orbit:**
  * **Peter Thiel's contrarian bet:** In August 2008—when Silicon Valley VCs chased asset-light social apps like Facebook and Twitter and ignored hard tech—old friend and rival Peter Thiel's Founders Fund stepped in to lead a $20 million round.
  * **First orbital success:** On September 28, 2008, Falcon 1's fourth flight reached orbit with a 165 kg metal mass simulator (which stayed in orbit for nearly 15 years before reentering), becoming the first privately developed orbital rocket in history.
  * **NASA's buzzer-beater rescue:** Lehman Brothers collapsed two weeks before that launch, freezing global liquidity. Musk sold houses and sports cars and even borrowed money to pay rent. By late December, SpaceX had cash for just one more payroll. On December 23, 2008, NASA called to award SpaceX a **$1.6 billion** CRS-1 cargo resupply contract, pulling the company back from the cliff.

## 3. Setting the Record Straight: Did NASA Really Favor SpaceX?

The documentary directly addresses claims that NASA played favorites with SpaceX:

* **Historical context and a rule change:** In 2005, knowing the Space Shuttle would retire in 2010, facing flat budgets, and reluctant to rely indefinitely on Russian spacecraft, NASA launched Commercial Orbital Transportation Services (COTS) and later CRS-1. It broke with legacy cost-plus contracting in favor of **fixed-price competitive bidding**: deliver cargo to the International Space Station, and you get paid.
* **Cultural hedge and dual-sourcing logic:** As Lewis explains, SpaceX embraces "fail fast and break rockets," whereas NASA lives by "Failure is not an option." NASA's contracting philosophy therefore avoids putting all eggs in one basket, funding at least two providers simultaneously—one steady, slower incumbent and one hungry newcomer.
* **Why SpaceX won:** Twenty-one companies bid in the initial round. Rejected proposals fell roughly into four buckets: legacy incumbents like Boeing/Lockheed repackaging old rockets; proposals directly importing Russian hardware; overly speculative concepts (such as air-towed rockets or modified "space minivans"); and pure PowerPoint pitches. Both finalists—SpaceX and Rocketplane Kistler (K1)—proposed homegrown reusable designs. In fact, K1, staffed by former NASA veterans, initially scored higher on schedule and technical readiness than SpaceX, yet failed to raise capital during the financial crisis without a founder willing to self-fund like Musk. NASA terminated K1 in 2007 and brought in Orbital Sciences as the replacement.
* **Contract sizes compared:** Of the $3.5 billion CRS-1 total, Orbital Sciences won $1.9 billion and SpaceX received $1.6 billion—the smaller share. Contract in hand, Musk canceled the planned Falcon 5 and jumped straight to the nine-engine Falcon 9. On its first operational CRS-1 mission in October 2012, an engine exploded 79 seconds after liftoff; flight computers shut it down cleanly and pushed the remaining engines to orbit, delivering 400 kg of supplies (including chocolate-vanilla ice cream for astronauts) and returning 700 kg of experiments.

The crew also visited SpaceX's Los Angeles headquarters—converted from a Boeing 747 parts plant in 2007 and used until the 2024 move to Texas—sitting right next to Tesla's global design studio, where Musk impulsively dug a hole in the employee parking lot and sparked The Boring Company. Lewis recounted Musk's relentless weekly cadence back then: all-day meetings at SpaceX on Monday, evening design reviews at Tesla before flying from the adjacent airport to Tesla's factory; Tuesday and Wednesday at Tesla; flying back for another full day of SpaceX reviews on Thursday; and weekends flying abroad (such as Australia) to meet officials—running seven days a week nonstop.

## 4. Radical Cost-Cutting and Internal Engineering Philosophy

In Musk's view, the biggest barrier to Mars is neither physics nor regulation—it is **cost**. SpaceX forged a ruthless engineering methodology around driving prices down:

### 1. The "Idiot Index" and the "Model S Index"

Musk used the **Idiot Index** (total component cost ÷ raw material cost) to expose bloated designs or inefficient manufacturing, and frequently compared the price of a Merlin engine against the cost of building an entire Tesla Model S. Engineers were ordered to slash component costs by 80% or risk losing their jobs. The documentary highlights several industry-shaking examples:

| Aerospace Component | Legacy Quote / Approach | SpaceX's Radical Redesign | Final Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Engine nozzle actuator** | Supplier quoted $120,000 | Repurposed a car-wash detergent mixing valve | ~$5,000 |
| **Spacecraft door latch** | NASA original price $1,500 | Adapted a bathroom stall latch | $30 |
| **Payload fairing air-cooling system** | Traditional system $3,000,000 | Modified commercial/home HVAC units & pumps | $6,000 |
| **Specialty crane** | $2,000,000 under old Air Force rules | Persuaded the Air Force to update outdated safety rules | $300,000 |

Through discipline like this, the build cost of a brand-new Falcon 9 was pushed down to roughly **$50 million**, 30%–50% below comparable vehicles on the market.

### 2. The Five-Step Algorithm and Communication Culture

In production meetings, Musk repeated his **five-step engineering algorithm** like a mantra:

1. **Question every requirement:** Always attach a person's name to a requirement and challenge its validity—especially when it comes from a smart person.
2. **Delete unnecessary parts and processes:** If you do not end up adding back at least 10% of what you cut, you did not delete enough.
3. **Simplify and optimize:** Only after deleting—because the single most common mistake of a smart engineer is optimizing something that should not exist.
4. **Accelerate cycle time:** Speed up only after completing steps 1 through 3.
5. **Automate last:** Never automate a redundant process.

Lewis added that a classic flaw among strong engineers is inventing nonexistent constraints and boxing themselves in. As for Musk's reputation for being autocratic, Lewis called it inaccurate: Musk listens closely to logic and hard evidence, and will accept being proved wrong. The one unforgivable sin inside SpaceX, however, is **declaring "that's impossible" without evidence**—anyone who commits it gets fired immediately regardless of seniority.

## 5. Cracking Rocket Reuse: A Darker Abyss Than Falcon 1

Musk liked to compare expendable rockets to aviation: if airlines threw away a Boeing 747 after every flight, no one could afford a ticket.

* **From "Grasshopper" to exploding barges:** In 2012, the team tested vertical takeoff and landing with a four-legged metal cylinder called *Grasshopper*, progressing from 1.8-meter hops to ascending 250 meters, translating 100 meters sideways, and touching down squarely on the X target. Moving to real orbital boosters in 2014–2015 brought repeated tip-overs at sea and fiery explosions on autonomous drone ships (which Musk wryly called "rapid unscheduled disassembly"), culminating in a major June 2015 mid-air breakup that halted recovery attempts.
* **An insider's darkest stretch:** As Falcon 9 chief engineer at the time, Lewis admitted **2015–2016 was even closer to existential collapse than the Falcon 1 years**. By then, SpaceX's scale and burn rate were far too big for Musk to cover out of pocket; the company was already ahead of NASA and the rest of humanity with no playbook to copy, while rivals aggressively poached staff. With the failure hitting his own division, Lewis said the team didn't even have the luxury to grieve, cry, or quit—unlike Blue Origin, there was no patron writing a $1 billion check every year. Failure meant the immediate end of the company.
* **Musk's unifying commitment:** At peak pressure, finger-pointing never took hold. Musk gave engineering teams room to solve the problem and backed them all-out—one Sunday night when a critical part had to get from Florida to California urgently, Musk instantly turned his brand-new, snow-white-interior private jet parked in Florida into a cargo plane. At an all-hands meeting, he made one simple pledge: "I will sell everything I own, even borrow money, and put my very last dollar into this company." Because everyone believed he meant every word, the entire workforce went all in.
* **Rewriting history:** On December 21, 2015, Falcon 9 achieved the first land-based orbital booster recovery in history (the documentary crew filmed that exact booster standing outside HQ). In April 2016 came the first sea landing on the autonomous drone ship *Of Course I Still Love You*, followed in February 2018 by Falcon Heavy's jaw-dropping dual-booster synchronized landing. Today Falcon 9 recovery success approaches 100%, turnaround times are under 20 days, and on February 21, 2026 a single first stage set a new world record of **33 flights and landings**—shattering the industry's former assumption of a 10-flight physical ceiling, with Musk projecting eventual lifetimes above 100 flights.

## 6. Commercial Flywheels: Starlink, Crew Dragon, and Starshield

### 1. Starlink: The Cash-Flow Engine Funding Mars

The entire global commercial launch market amounts to only tens of billions of dollars a year—too small to fund Mars even with a 100% monopoly. Musk targeted the $1 trillion global telecom market instead: capturing just 3% delivers $30 billion in annual revenue, surpassing NASA's entire yearly budget.

* **Scale today:** Since launching its first test satellites in 2018, Starlink now operates over **10,000 active satellites in orbit** (roughly 65% of all active satellites worldwide), exceeds 10 million subscribers globally, and has expanded aggressively across aviation (Hawaiian Airlines, Qatar Airways, Air New Zealand, etc.) and cruise lines (Royal Caribbean, Norwegian, MSC Cruises, etc.).
* **Financial and strategic closed loop:** According to the prospectus, Starlink generated **$11.4 billion** in 2025 revenue—**61%** of SpaceX's total revenue. Crucially, 123 of SpaceX's 165 launches in 2025 (74.5%) carried its own Starlink satellites. With a planned 42,000-satellite constellation and 10%–15% annual replenishment, SpaceX built an internal loop no competitor can steal: high launch cadence amortizes R&D and manufacturing costs while providing massive real-world flight data to iterate rockets faster.

### 2. Crew Dragon: A Textbook Rout of Boeing

Under NASA's Commercial Crew Program (CCP), Boeing received the lion's share at $4.2 billion while SpaceX got $2.6 billion. SpaceX stripped away dense banks of mechanical switches in favor of three crisp touchscreens and vision-based autonomous docking. Boeing's Starliner, by contrast, suffered thruster malfunctions and helium leaks in 2024 that left two astronauts stranded on the ISS for over nine months until Crew Dragon brought them home—effectively shelving Boeing's crewed program indefinitely and leaving Crew Dragon as America's de facto sole crewed spacecraft.

* **How to persuade Elon Musk:** Lewis shared an anecdote from designing Crew Dragon's cockpit. Musk initially insisted on zero physical buttons, just like a Tesla Model S. Convinced that carried unnecessary safety risk, the engineering team built a deck with two punchy executive summary slides backed by 50–60 pages of supporting data, laying out three options (traditional button-heavy cockpit, touchscreen plus a few emergency physical switches, and pure touch/voice) with honest cost estimates—winning Musk over with engineering data to accept the optimal hybrid with emergency physical switches.
* **Commercial human spaceflight milestones:** In 2021, *Inspiration4* completed the first all-civilian orbital flight; in 2024, *Polaris Dawn* conducted the first commercial spacewalk and reached an altitude of 1,400 km, the farthest humans have traveled from Earth since Apollo 17 in 1972.

### 3. Starshield: The Defense Pillar Behind a Trillion-Dollar Valuation

SpaceX is now embedded across virtually every flagship NASA program (lunar landers, lunar logistics, ISS deorbiting) while scaling rapidly in national defense. Roughly 25% of SpaceX's 2025 launches were classified. Its dedicated government and defense brand, **Starshield**, spans three satellite lines:

1. **Encrypted military communications satellites:** Built on mature Starlink V2/V3 production lines, upgraded with anti-jam antennas, military-grade encryption, and laser links.
2. **Modular "Lego-style" satellite buses:** SpaceX supplies power, propulsion, and navigation platforms onto which defense agencies plug high-resolution imaging or signals-intelligence payloads.
3. **Proprietary non-communications satellites:** Including newly deployed missile-tracking satellites monitoring global ICBMs and hypersonic vehicles.

Backed by long-term, exclusive, prepaid contracts with margins far above commercial services, SpaceX secured a $1.8 billion NRO real-time imaging spy-satellite contract, a 480-satellite dedicated Space Force communications architecture, and a $6.45 billion Space Force contract in late May—forming a cornerstone of its trillion-dollar valuation.

## 7. The Ultimate Vehicle "Starship" and the Starbase Spaceport

Musk calculates that building a self-sustaining city on Mars requires shipping at least **1 million tons** of cargo upfront. Lewis drew a ground-transport analogy: Rocket Lab is a Ferrari, Falcon 9 is a semi-truck, and **Starship is the freight train to space**—delivering an order-of-magnitude leap in payload capacity and engineered from day one for **full and rapid reusability**, targeting re-flight within an hour of landing.

### 1. Starship's Two Foundational Breakthroughs

* **The Raptor engine:** Accounts for roughly half of Starship's total engineering effort.
  * *Liquid oxygen + liquid methane propellant:* First, Mars has carbon dioxide and water ice, enabling in-situ methane production; second, unlike Falcon 9's kerosene—which leaves heavy coking soot requiring ten-plus days of cleaning—methane burns extraordinarily clean for rapid turnaround; third, liquid methane and liquid oxygen sit at similar cryogenic temperatures, eliminating thick insulating bulkheads and saving major mass.
  * *Full-flow staged combustion cycle:* Delivers peak thrust-to-weight and fuel efficiency, achieving mass-produced flight for the first time while operating temperatures and pressures push the physical edge of materials science.
  * *Blistering iteration speed:* Evolved from V1 to V3 in just over two years, internalizing hundreds of external plumbing lines while slashing engine mass from 2 tons to 1.5 tons and surging thrust from 180 tons to 270 tons.
* **Stainless-steel airframe:** Musk scrapped carbon fiber at the eleventh hour for stainless steel. Its advantages: drastically cheaper; no need to spend years and hundreds of millions on climate-controlled cleanrooms (you can start welding under a tent); high-temperature resilience (retaining structural strength at 800°C and drastically reducing heavy, fragile heat-shield tiles); and cryogenic performance (yield and tensile strength actually increase by over 50% below −150°C).

### 2. Starship Flight Milestones and the Arrival of V3

From 2019's open-air welded test article *Starhopper*, to upper-stage prototypes conquering the 10 km belly-flop and flip-landing maneuver on the seventh attempt after multiple explosions, to the April 2023 full-stack maiden flight that pulverized the launch pad and the October 2024 fifth flight's iconic mid-air booster catch by the launch tower's "chopsticks." In May 2026, the stretched **Starship V3** powered by Raptor 3 engines debuted with nearly 9,000 tons of total liftoff thrust and close to 100 tons of single-flight payload capacity.

### 3. On the Ground at Starbase, the "Spaceport"

The crew toured Boca Chica on the Texas–Mexico border—the southernmost point in the continental U.S. closest to the equator, just two blocks across from Mexico:

* **Gigafactory-to-pad integration:** Parts manufactured in the rear factory halls move into towering black Mega Bays for stacking, then roll directly down the road to the launch pad to be lifted by the chopsticks—an end-to-end closed loop inside a single campus. Lewis predicts rows of launch towers lining the coast like airport gates within 5–10 years.
* **Border town transformation and incorporation:** Once a desolate fishing and hunting stretch with neither a post office nor a mayor in the 2010s, the influx of engineers and visitors brought Japanese cuisine, ramen, French restaurants, and a "SpaceX Bar" for milestone celebrations. Starbase has now incorporated as a municipality, enabling employees to participate directly in local governance.
* **Nationwide replication and local friction:** Starbase is only the blueprint. SpaceX is scouting sites in Louisiana and elsewhere to replicate its "Spaceport" model nationwide like Tesla Gigafactories, working toward Musk's goal of **1,000 launches per year** (current scale is less than one-tenth of that target). The documentary also interviews local residents candidly: launches rattle the ground and roar across 20–30 miles, and explosions raise air-quality concerns, leaving an ongoing tension between base expansion and local environmental stewardship.

## 8. Epilogue: The IPO Is Only the Beginning, and Mars Is Not the Finish Line

In closing, the documentary notes that even though colonizing Mars reflects an intensely personal vision, SpaceX's drastic reduction in launch costs is already reshaping life on Earth:

* Remote villages deep in the Amazon rainforest and crews aboard ocean freighters have high-speed internet for the first time via Starlink;
* Varda Space has manufactured crystals of the HIV drug ritonavir in orbital microgravity;
* SpaceX is preparing a private GPS alternative that is more accurate and less expensive than the U.S. government system;
* Redwire has drawn defect-free, ultra-high-purity optical fibers in zero gravity.

SpaceX has never seen itself as merely a rocket maker or tech company; its ultimate mandate is to **make life multiplanetary**—with the Moon and Mars only the first waystations toward the wider cosmos. Looking ahead at the end of the episode, Lewis Hong conservatively estimates humans will set foot on Mars **within 20 years**. Where the new space age meets the AI era, ideas that once sounded like pure science fiction are rapidly turning into tangible reality.

## 一、 创业缘起：从“行为艺术”到小仓库里的猎鹰 1 号

* **“火星绿洲”与赴俄受辱**：SpaceX 的起点最初带有行为艺术色彩。马斯克浏览 NASA 官网时惊讶地发现，人类登月后竟然连重返月球和登陆火星的计划都没有。他最初打算买一枚退役的苏联洲际导弹，把一个小温室送上火星拍照，借此激起公众热情、倒逼政府增加 NASA 预算（即“火星绿洲”计划）。为此他三次前往俄罗斯谈判，却遭对方漫天要价，甚至有一位俄罗斯火箭设计师朝他的皮鞋吐口水，认为这个美国来的富豪根本不懂火箭。
* **飞机上的第一性原理测算**：在返美飞机上，马斯克用 Excel 核算了铝合金、钛金属、碳纤维、推进剂等火箭原材料的市场价，发现材料成本仅占火箭总售价的约 **3%**。他意识到，火箭天价的罪魁祸首并非纯科学难题，而是官僚机构低效、冗长供应链以及波音、洛克希德·马丁等传统军工巨头习惯的“成本加成”模式。于是 2002 年他用 PayPal 套现的资金创立了 SpaceX（成立时间甚至早于 Facebook）。
* **小仓库与荒岛求生**：节目组实地探访了 SpaceX 位于洛杉矶机场旁的第一个小仓库（如今已变成一家海鲜交易公司）。Lewis 回忆，公司最初就是从马斯克和一号员工、著名发动机专家汤姆·穆勒（Tom Mueller）两人起步，整个猎鹰 1 号火箭都是在这个并不大的厂房里开发出来的。而发射测试则在太平洋马绍尔群岛一座仅 0.03 平方公里的荒岛上进行，岛上既无淡水也无像样宿舍，高浓度海风盐雾让工程师不得不反复清洁和检查金属零件。

## 二、 猎鹰 1 号三连败与绝处逢生（2006–2008）

马斯克最初的策略是先造小型火箭发射小卫星，再逐步迭代。但看似像“玩具”的猎鹰 1 号，却让团队在生死线上挣扎了数年：

1. **第一次失败（2006 年 3 月）**：起飞几十秒后坠毁。因热带海岛盐分极高，燃料管路的一个螺母被腐蚀出微裂纹导致漏油熄火。Lewis 坦言，当时大家确实不懂造火箭，但团队学习极快，立刻重新改造了整个一级推进管路。
2. **第二次失败（2007 年 3 月）**：一级火箭表现完美，但在一二级分离后，二级姿态控制出问题并产生剧烈震动，发动机提前熄火未能入轨。
3. **第三次失败（2008 年 8 月）**：最令团队心碎的一次。前两次的问题已全部解决，却栽在分离时序上——二级分离慢了一秒，一级火箭的余力向前撞击了二级，导致火箭失控坠海，连同 NASA 小卫星和 208 人的太空葬骨灰一同沉入太平洋。Lewis 感慨，第三枚火箭已是 99% 的成品，但在航天领域 99% 远远不够，必须做到 100%。

* **至暗时刻与即兴演说**：三连败后，PayPal 套现的钱几乎耗尽，叠加 2008 年金融危机、特斯拉濒临倒闭、马斯克深陷离婚官司，监控室死寂一片，有人失声痛哭。马斯克随即发表即兴演讲，承诺“我绝不会放弃，永远都不会”，并宣布已凑到第四次发射资金，号召大家回到工位找问题。
* **两笔“救命钱”与历史性首飞**：
  * **彼得·蒂尔逆势下注**：2008 年 8 月，正值硅谷 VC 狂热追捧 Facebook、Twitter 等轻资产社交软件、对硬科技无人问津之际，老友兼老对手彼得·蒂尔旗下的 Founders Fund 逆势独家领投 2000 万美元。
  * **首飞成功**：2008 年 9 月 28 日，猎鹰 1 号第四次发射成功，将 165 公斤金属配重送入轨道（在轨运行近 15 年才坠毁），成为人类史上首枚由私营公司成功发射的轨道级火箭。
  * **NASA 压哨救援**：发射成功前两周雷曼兄弟破产，全球流动性枯竭。马斯克卖掉房产和跑车，甚至借钱交房租。到圣诞前夕，公司账上只够再发一次工资。2008 年 12 月 23 日，NASA 致电授予 SpaceX 价值 **16 亿美元**的 CRS-1 货运合同，将公司从悬崖边拉回。

## 三、 深度还原：NASA 真的偏袒 SpaceX 吗？

视频专门厘清了外界关于“NASA 偏袒 SpaceX”的争议：

* **时代背景与规则剧变**：2005 年，在确定航天飞机将于 2010 年退役、预算多年停滞且不愿长期依赖俄罗斯飞船的背景下，NASA 启动了商业轨道运输招标（COTS 及其后的 CRS-1），打破了传统“成本加成”模式，改为**固定价格公开招标**——谁把货送到国际空间站，谁拿钱。
* **文化对冲与双保险逻辑**：Lewis 指出，SpaceX 的文化是“快速试错、允许炸机”，而 NASA 是“绝不允许失败（Failure is not an option）”。因此 NASA 发包的核心逻辑是从不把鸡蛋放在一个篮子里，至少同时扶植两家以上：一家是效率不高但稳妥的老牌承包商，另一家则是给新锐公司机会。
* **为什么是 SpaceX 中标**：首轮共有 21 家公司竞标，落选者大致分四类：波音/洛马等“换汤不换药”改旧火箭的；直接引进俄罗斯火箭飞船的；方案过于超前风险极大的（如飞机空中拖火箭、魔改“太空面包车”）；以及纯 PPT 公司。最终入围的 SpaceX 和 Rocketplane Kistler（K1）都是自研可回收方案。事实上，由前 NASA 班底组成的 K1 早期进度和技术评分甚至高于 SpaceX，但因金融危机融不到钱且无人像马斯克那样自掏腰包，最终在 2007 年被撤单，由轨道科学公司补位。
* **合同金额对比**：CRS-1 总计 35 亿美元，轨道科学拿了 19 亿，SpaceX 拿了 16 亿，并非大头。拿下订单后，马斯克果断砍掉原定的猎鹰 5 号，直接研发配备 9 台发动机的猎鹰 9 号。2012 年 10 月首次执行 CRS-1 补给任务时，起飞 79 秒即有一台发动机爆炸，但机载电脑果断关机并依靠其余发动机成功入轨，把 400 公斤补给（包括给宇航员的巧克力香草冰淇淋）送达空间站，并带回 700 公斤实验样本。

节目组还实地探访了 SpaceX 于 2007 年接手波音 747 零件厂房建成的洛杉矶总部（一直使用到 2024 年迁往德州）。这里紧邻特斯拉全球设计总部，马斯克还曾突发奇想在员工停车场直接挖洞，催生了 Boring Company。Lewis 分享了马斯克当年近乎疯魔的固定作息：周一在 SpaceX 开一整天会、晚上去特斯拉设计部看图后直接从隔壁机场飞往特斯拉工厂；周二周三在特斯拉办公；周四飞回 SpaceX 再开一整天会；周末飞去澳洲等地见政要，一周七天连轴转。

## 四、 极致降本与内部管理哲学

在马斯克看来，去火星最大的障碍不是技术或监管，而是**太贵**。SpaceX 围绕降本建立了一套极其严苛的工程方法论：

### 1. “白痴指数”与“Model S 指数”

马斯克用白痴指数（零部件总成本 ÷ 原材料成本）衡量设计是否过于复杂或制造低效，并常拿造一辆特斯拉 Model S 的成本去质问高价的梅林发动机。他要求工程师将负责的部件成本直接砍掉 80%，做不到就可能面临解雇。视频列举了多个颠覆行业的降本实例：

| 航天部件 | 传统报价 / 方案 | SpaceX 的极致改造方案 | 最终成本 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **发动机喷管驱动器** | 供应商报价 12 万美元 | 改造洗车系统混合清洁液的阀门 | 约 5000 美元 |
| **空间站门栓** | NASA 原价 1500 美元 | 改造浴室隔间门插销 | 30 美元 |
| **载荷舱空气冷却系统** | 传统方案 300 万美元 | 改造家用空调系统与泵 | 6000 美元 |
| **特种吊车** | 按空军旧条例需 200 万美元 | 说服空军修改过时安全条例 | 30 万美元 |

通过这类举措，全新一枚猎鹰 9 号的造价被压低至约 **5000 万美元**，比市场同类产品低 30% 至 50%。

### 2. 五步工作法与内部沟通文化

马斯克在生产会议上像念咒一样反复强调**五步工作法**：

1. **质疑每项要求**：必须问出提出者的名字，无论对方多聪明都要质疑其合理性。
2. **删除多余部件与流程**：如果事后加回来的部分不到删除量的 10%，说明删得还不够狠。
3. **简化与优化**：必须在删除之后做，因为工程师最常犯的错就是去优化一个本不该存在的东西。
4. **加快周转时间**：在前三步完成之后再提速。
5. **最后才是自动化**：切忌把冗余流程直接自动化。

Lewis 补充道，优秀工程师最大的通病是“预设不存在的问题、为自己设限”。至于外界传言马斯克独断专行，Lewis 认为并不准确：马斯克其实非常听得进逻辑与证据，只要你能证明他的想法不成立，他会接受；但他在体系内绝不容忍的“死罪”是**毫无根据地直接说“这不可能”**，犯此大忌者无论资历多深都会被立刻解雇。

## 五、 死磕火箭回收：比猎鹰 1 号更绝望的至暗时刻

马斯克曾打比方：如果每坐一次飞机就要扔掉一架波音 747，机票得卖到天价。

* **从“蚱蜢”到连环炸船**：2012 年，团队用四条腿的铁罐子“蚱蜢（Grasshopper）”验证垂直起降，从最初跳高 1.8 米，一路做到升空 250 米、横向平移 100 米后再精准落回 X 标记。但 2014 至 2015 年进入真实火箭回收测试后，猎鹰 9 号屡次在海面倾覆或在无人驳船上炸成火球（马斯克自嘲为“快速非计划性解体”），直到 2015 年 6 月发生首次空中解体重大事故，回收被迫中断。
* **亲历者的至暗回忆**：作为当时猎鹰 9 号首席工程师，Lewis 坦言 **2015–2016 年其实比猎鹰 1 号时期更濒临绝境**。当年公司体量和烧钱速度已大到无法靠马斯克借钱填坑，而且 SpaceX 已经跑在 NASA 和全人类前面，没人能教你怎么办，竞争对手还在趁机疯狂挖角。由于事故发生在自己部门，团队每天连“伤心、哭泣或放弃的资格都没有”，因为背后没有像蓝色起源那样每年白给 10 亿美元的金主，做不出来公司就地终结。
* **马斯克的凝聚力**：在最高压的时刻，公司内部没有互相推诿，马斯克不仅给予工程团队充分空间，更展现极强的支持力——某周日晚团队急需把佛州的一个关键火箭部件运回加州测试，马斯克二话不说把停在佛州、内饰雪白的全新私人飞机直接改成了“货机”。他在全员大会上简单表态：“我会卖掉全部资产，哪怕借钱也会把最后一分钱投进这家公司。”因为全员深信他真会这么做，整个团队彻底拼了命。
* **改写历史**：2015 年 12 月 21 日，猎鹰 9 号完成人类史上首次轨道级火箭陆上回收（节目组在总部外拍到了这枚真正的原箭）；2016 年 4 月完成首次海上无人驳船“当然我还爱你号（Of Course I Still Love You）”回收；2018 年 2 月重型猎鹰双助推器同步垂直着陆震撼全球。如今猎鹰 9 号回收成功率接近 100%，周转时间缩至 20 天以内；2026 年 2 月 21 日，单枚一级火箭刚刷新了 **33 次**发射与回收的世界纪录（打破业界曾认为的 10 次物理极限，马斯克预测最终寿命可达 100 次以上）。

## 六、 商业飞轮：星链、载人飞船与星盾

### 1. 星链（Starlink）：支撑火星梦的现金流引擎

全球商业发射市场一年仅几百亿美元，就算全垄断也不够去火星。马斯克瞄准了规模达 1 万亿美元的全球通信市场——只要拿下 3% 份额就是 300 亿美元年收入，超过 NASA 全年预算。

* **现状与体量**：自 2018 年发射首批实验星以来，星链目前在轨卫星已超 **1 万颗**（占全球活跃卫星约 65%），全球订阅用户突破千万，并大举切入航空（夏威夷航空、卡塔尔航空、新西兰航空等）与远洋邮轮市场（皇家加勒比、挪威人、MSC 地中海等）。
* **财务与战略闭环**：据招股书，星链 2025 年营收达 **114 亿美元**，占 SpaceX 总营收的 **61%**。更关键的是，2025 年 SpaceX 全年 165 次发射中有 123 次（占比 74.5%）是在打自家星链卫星。按 4.2 万颗规划及每年 10%–15% 的换代需求，这构成了对手抢不走的“内循环”：高频发射摊薄研发生产成本，同时提供海量试错机会加速火箭迭代。

### 2. 载人龙飞船（Crew Dragon）：完胜波音的教科书案例

在 NASA 商业载人计划（CCP）中，波音分得 42 亿美元大头，SpaceX 仅拿 26 亿美元。但 SpaceX 推出的载人龙飞船彻底抛弃了密密麻麻的物理开关，采用三块高清触控屏和视觉全自动对接系统。反观波音的 Starliner 飞船在 2024 年因推进器故障和氦气泄漏，导致两名宇航员滞留空间站长达 9 个多月，最终不得不由龙飞船接回，波音载人计划随之被无限期搁置，龙飞船成为美国事实上的唯一载人飞船。

* **说服马斯克的实战技巧**：Lewis 分享了当年设计龙飞船驾驶舱的趣事。马斯克最初执意要像特斯拉 Model S 一样“一个按钮都不留”，团队认为存在安全隐患且极度没必要。于是团队准备了“前两页直击重点 + 后附五六十页数据支撑”的演示文稿，并列出传统多按钮版、折中触控+少量应急按钮版、纯触控语音版三个方案及各自高昂的成本报价，成功用工程数据说服马斯克接受了保留应急物理按钮的最优折中方案。
* **商业载人里程碑**：2021 年 Inspiration4 实现人类首次全平民绕地飞行；2024 年北极星黎明（Polaris）任务实现人类首次商业太空行走，并飞抵 1400 公里轨道高度，创下 1972 年阿波罗 17 号以来人类飞离地球的最远纪录。

### 3. 星盾（Starshield）：万亿估值背后的国防支柱

目前 SpaceX 已渗透进 NASA 几乎所有核心项目（登月着陆器、月球物流、国际空间站退役脱轨等），更在国防领域迅速崛起。2025 年 SpaceX 约有 25% 的发射任务高度保密，旗下政府与国防专属品牌**星盾**主要包含三类卫星：

1. **加密军用通信卫星**：基于成熟的星链 V2/V3 产线，加装抗干扰天线、军用级加密模块与激光链路。
2. **乐高式卫星总线**：SpaceX 只提供动力与导航基座，军方自行插接高分辨率相机或电子侦听载荷。
3. **自研非通信卫星**：如已开始部署的导弹追踪卫星，专门盯防全球洲际导弹与高超音速飞行器。

凭借长期排他、预付且利润率远高于民用业务的特性，SpaceX 接连斩获美国国家侦察局 18 亿美元实时成像间谍卫星订单、太空军 480 颗专用通信卫星体系，以及 5 月下旬来自太空军的 64.5 亿美元新合同，成为支撑其万亿估值的关键板块。

## 七、 终极兵器“星舰”与 Starbase 太空港口

马斯克测算，要在火星建立自给自足的城市，前期至少需要运送 **100 万吨**物资。Lewis 用地面交通工具做类比：Rocket Lab 像法拉利，猎鹰 9 号像卡车，而**星舰（Starship）就是通往太空的火车**——运力呈数十倍跃升，且从设计第一天起就追求“完全且快速复用（Rapidly Reusable）”，目标是降落一小时就能再次起飞。

### 1. 星舰的两大底层创新

* **猛禽（Raptor）发动机**：占星舰约一半的研发工作量。
  * *液氧甲烷燃料*：第一，火星有二氧化碳和水冰，可通过化学反应就地制取甲烷；第二，相比猎鹰 9 号的煤油会产生大量积碳导致回收后要清洗十几天，甲烷燃烧极干净，支持快速复飞；第三，液态甲烷与液氧温度接近，省去厚重隔热隔板，大幅减重。
  * *全流量分级燃烧循环*：推重比和燃料效率极致，首次实现量产上天，内部温压已逼近材料学物理极限。
  * *恐怖的迭代速度*：仅用两年多从 V1 迭代到 V3，外部成百上千根管线几乎完全内化消失，单台重量从 2 吨降至 1.5 吨，推力从 180 吨暴涨至 270 吨。
* **不锈钢箭体**：马斯克在最后时刻推翻碳纤维方案改用不锈钢。优势在于：极度便宜；无需花费数年和数亿美元建恒温恒湿洁净厂房，搭个帐篷就能开造；耐高温（800 度仍保持结构强度，大幅省去笨重且难维护的隔热瓦）且耐超低温（在零下 150 度以下的燃料温度中，屈服强度和抗拉强度反而提升 50% 以上）。

### 2. 星舰测试历程与 V3 登场

从 2019 年露天焊接的早期验证机“星虫（Starhopper）”试跳，到二级火箭经历多次爆炸后在第七次测试攻克 10 公里高空“腹式下落与拉直降落”；再到 2023 年 4 月完整体首飞震碎发射台、2024 年 10 月第五次测试实现发射塔“筷子”空中夹住一级助推器的史诗级画面。2026 年 5 月，换装猛禽三代发动机、箭体加长的 **V3 版星舰**正式登场，总推力近 9000 吨，具备单次近百吨有效载荷的运力。

### 3. 实地探访 Starbase“太空港口”

节目组走进位于德州美墨边境的 Boca Chica（美国最南端、距赤道最近，走两个街区对面就是墨西哥）。

* **超级工厂+发射一体化**：后方大厂房生产零件 -> 运入巨大的黑色 Mega Bay 组装塔合体 -> 直接沿道路拉到发射台由“筷子”吊装发射，全部在同一园区闭环完成。Lewis 预测，未来 5–10 年内这里将像机场停机坪一样排满成排的发射塔。
* **边城蜕变与自治市**：这片 2010 年代还无邮局、无市长的荒凉渔猎地带，因大量工程师和游客涌入，冒出了日料、拉面、法餐和供团队庆祝重大里程碑的“SpaceX 酒吧”。如今 Starbase 已获得自治市资格，方便员工直接参与市政管理。
* **全国复制与现实摩擦**：Starbase 只是样板，SpaceX 已在路易斯安那州等地考察选址，准备把“太空港（Spaceport）”模式像特斯拉超级工厂一样复制到全美，以支撑马斯克“年发射 1000 次”的目标（目前规模还不到未来的十分之一）。不过视频也客观采访了当地居民：火箭发射时方圆二三十英里地面震动、巨响轰鸣，加上炸箭对空气质量的影响，基地扩张与社区环保之间仍需寻找平衡。

## 八、 结语：IPO 只是开始，火星也不是终点

视频最后指出，尽管“殖民火星”带有强烈的个人理想色彩，但 SpaceX 极度拉低太空准入门槛的红利已经真实改变了地球生活：

* 亚马逊雨林深处的村庄和远洋货轮上的船员，第一次靠星链连上了高速网络；
* Varda Space 在太空微重力环境下制造出了治疗艾滋病药物利托那韦的晶体；
* SpaceX 正在筹建比美国政府更精准、更便宜的私营版 GPS；
* Redwire 已在无重力环境下拉制出完全无缺陷的高纯度光纤。

对 SpaceX 而言，它从不只是一家火箭公司或科技公司，其终极宗旨是让人类成为**跨行星物种（Make life multiplanetary）**——月球和火星只是第一站，终极目标是整个宇宙。在片尾的展望中，Lewis Hong 保守预计人类将在 **20 年内**登陆火星；大航天时代与大 AI 时代的交汇，正在把曾经的天方夜谭变成触手可及的现实。
